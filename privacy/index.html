Framelet Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Framelet is a Mac app for browsing, organizing and cleaning up folders of photos and videos. It is built so that your photos stay on your Mac.

What Framelet collects

Nothing. Framelet doesn’t collect, store or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporting of its own.

Your photos and videos

  • Framelet only opens folders you choose. macOS enforces this through the App Sandbox.
  • Everything Framelet does with your photos happens on your Mac: thumbnails, search by content, categories, duplicate detection, Enhance Photo and Extract Subject all use the on-device frameworks built into macOS. No photo, video, file name or analysis result is sent anywhere.
  • To stay fast, Framelet keeps three caches in its own folder on your Mac: thumbnails, photo analysis and capture dates. They never contain file names. Settings ▸ Storage shows where each one is kept, and you can clear them there at any time.
  • Favorites and labels are saved as Finder tags on your files, so they stay with your files.
  • The bottom bar shows how much space is free on the disk your folder is on, and how much you moved to the Trash today. Framelet reads the free space from macOS only to show it to you, and keeps today’s total (one number and the date) in its own settings. Neither leaves your Mac.
  • A copy saved from Enhance or Extract Subject (or a photo Enhance replaced) gets a small hidden file attribute saying which tool made it (enhanced, subject or sticker), so the grid can mark it as edited. It holds nothing else and never leaves your Mac.

Internet use

Framelet works offline. It goes online only for:

  • The map view, which loads map tiles from Apple Maps. Framelet never uploads your photos or their GPS coordinates. As with any map, though, Apple receives requests for the map area you are looking at, and that area can be where a photo was taken. Map requests are covered by Apple’s privacy policy.
  • Follow Roads on the map, only if you turn it on (it’s off by default). To draw the journey along real roads, Framelet asks Apple Maps for directions between each pair of stops, which sends those two places (the average location of the photos at each stop) to Apple. No photos, file names or dates are sent. Turn it off and nothing more is asked; roads already found are kept in memory only until you open another folder.
  • Framelet Pro, which is bought and restored through the Mac App Store. Apple handles the payment; Framelet never sees your payment details or Apple Account information.

Children

Framelet doesn’t collect data from anyone, including children.
